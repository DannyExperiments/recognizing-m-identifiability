# Q403: O*(3^m) exact algorithm for m latent factors

After removing already certified factors, fix a pivot v with unique remaining parent h. By the delivered min-cut lemma, it suffices to find an exact matching using h, meaning every common parent of the two disjoint selected sides is used. Enumerate three labels of each residual latent: common, left-only, or right-only; force h common. For each common latent c make two slots (c,L),(c,R) and join each slot to observed children of c other than v which have no parent labeled only for the opposite side. Test for a matching saturating all slots against the observed nodes. Distinct matched representatives ensure the two sides are disjoint. All common labels appear on both sides and no labeled one-sided latent can be common. Conversely tag any exact witness by its genuinely common and one-sided parents (unused parents take either one-sided label), obtaining the matching. At most 3^(m−1) choices per pivot.

Deleting another latent g leaves the unique-parent requirement for h unchanged. If g is used, delete both its endpoints; otherwise retain the witness. No extra common parent can arise and each surviving used parent remains common. Thus any eligible pivot can be greedily peeled: in a full order, move the newly certified latent to the front and restrict every earlier witness to remaining factors. At most m rounds of pivot scans and polynomial matching checks give O*(3^m), with the polynomial in complete input size independent of the parameter. This addresses ordinary M-identifiability only. Compare the observed-subset enumeration in https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02986v2#A2.
