When the pivot certifies a factor

The base sides match through h and r. The pivot v certifies h when adding a copy of v to each side makes a full matching impossible.

Why the extra factor matters

Each path must use a distinct latent center. The two copies of v can only use h, so a full augmented matching must include v ← h → v. That leaves a, b, c, and d needing two other centers. With only r available they cannot match. With r and s they can.

In the enlarged graph v is the only observed vertex with a sole parent. Any exact witness for h must match a and b through h. This also makes r common, which would need c and d. Those endpoints make s common as well, with no endpoints left for it. Thus the enlarged graph has no first certificate.
