# Q403 source and priority assessment

**Verdict.** The handoff matches the ordinary, unrestricted recognition problem in one half of Conjecture 5.3. Moderate confidence in the bounded search for prior solutions. I found no earlier proof of the unrestricted ordinary NP-completeness classification. The graph criterion, witness checking, monotonicity and recursive peeling are established prior work.

## Exact source and recognition question

Nils Sturma, Miriam Kranzlmueller, Irem Portakal and Mathias Drton, *Matching Criterion for Identifiability in Sparse Factor Analysis*, **Psychometrika** 91(2), 536–555 (2026), DOI 10.1017/psy.2026.10079. The [published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13294627/) retains Conjecture 5.3. The active arXiv version is [v2, 9 January 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02986v2), and the open preprint gives the [full Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02986v2#A2). The pinned [catalog entry](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/mathematical-statistics.md#q403) is a secondary restatement.

Definition 4.1 uses paths between observed endpoints through distinct latent centers. In Definition 4.7 the pivot v is excluded from two disjoint nonempty observed sets W,U of equal size, and v has a sole remaining parent h after S is removed. A matching avoiding S must exist between W and U. No matching avoiding S may exist between the two augmented sets with v on both sides. In the residual graph each copy of v can only be matched through h, so any augmented matching must use the common path (v,h,v). The handoff uses the equivalent condition that the base sides match, but fail to match after h is deleted. Theorem 4.8 recursively certifies factors by the criterion. Algorithm 2 initializes childless factors as solved.

Conjecture 5.3 asks about both ordinary and extended M-identifiability with no constant bound on witness size. The extended criterion adds the local BB step in Definition 4.14, with joint parents defined by occurrence at least twice among the selected children. A single eligible ordinary pivot therefore does not exclude a different first move in the extended problem. Theorem 4.8 gives a sufficient criterion for generic sign-identifiability. Hardness of this recognition language does not imply hardness of deciding generic identifiability.

## Existing methods and overlap

- Sturma et al. Appendix B Lemma B.1 proves persistence of a certification when additional solved factors are removed. It deletes the paths centered at those factors. Lemma B.2 checks a supplied witness by max flow and already proves membership in NP for this local question. Lemma B.3 proves that h must be a common parent of both sides. Algorithms 1–2 enumerate observed subsets and greedily peel certifications. Theorem 5.1 treats bounded witness sides. Fixed witness-size bounds are outside the reduction’s promise. These ingredients and their attribution belong in the manuscript.
- The candidate uses a min cut to shrink both sides to a witness matching that uses exactly all common parents. The exactness forces every shared relay or lock center to be used, thereby enforcing the clause construction. The previously published local checking algorithm does not provide that normal form or a search hardness proof.
- The latent-parameter supplement searches three labels per factor and solves a bipartite representatives problem on two slots for each common factor. It should credit the already known peeling step. The exponential dependence on the number of latent factors is a different parameter statement from enumerating observed subsets at a fixed witness size. Hosszejni and Frühwirth-Schnatter use a duplicated latent bipartite graph to check the Anderson–Rubin criterion by matching, as described immediately before Definition 4.7 in Sturma et al. That is close algorithmic context for the slot construction and concerns a different criterion. [Source discussion and citation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02986v2#S4.SS1).
- Anderson–Rubin and Bekker–ten Berge criteria, ZUTA/full-ZUTA structure, and generic sign-identifiability are related questions. The published source compares them in §§3–4 and presents examples distinguishing the criteria. Those results do not decide the stated unrestricted recognition language.

Publications on matching for the other criteria were checked through the primary paper’s comparison. No absolute priority claim is made.
